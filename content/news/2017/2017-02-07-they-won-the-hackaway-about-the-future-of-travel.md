**A new app with personalised traffic information – that was the winner of an intense hackaway about the future of travel. The winning app only informs about the traffic disruptions that are relevant based on how we travel in everyday traffic. The event gathered some 40 developers from companies including Spotify and Mojang on Käringön in Bohuslän one weekend at the end of January. Besides the winning entry, lots of other ideas for web solutions and apps were born, all of them built on real data.**

![](/media/2017/02/2017-02-07-de-vann-hackaway-om-framtidens-resande-1.jpg)

The winners Max Schedin, Poyan Nabati and Tobias Ahlin of Team Ridepulse received the prize on 6 February during the Tech Meetup Stockholm event. The award ceremony was led by former Minister for Infrastructure Catharina Elmsäter Swärd. The travel hack was organised by the development company Nobina Technology, Confetti, Drive Sweden, Ericsson and Trafiklab.

– The participants were incredibly sharp and it was great to see such a wide range of solutions. There were no fake apps or services either; everything was built on real data from sources we already use, says Anna Haeger, business developer at Nobina Technology.

**Winning travel app competes in Montreal**

The winners in Team Ridepulse call their project ”Sam”, as in samåkning (carpooling). In the iOS app the group developed, you choose roughly when and how you want to travel and then receive information about any delays affecting your own route.

– Our idea helps everyone who uses public transport to get to and from work to avoid disruptions in traffic – and to reduce small moments of frustration in everyday life, says Tobias Ahlin, one of the winners, who works at Mojang in his day job.

– There is a lot of traffic information available, almost too much. But by focusing on where you live and work and your daily habits, we can filter out the irrelevant information and send you notifications if there are disruptions that affect you and your everyday life, Tobias continues.

The winning team now has a trip to Montreal on 15–17 May ahead of them, to represent Sweden at the global hack during the Global Public Transport Summit organised by UITP. Whether their app will be launched publicly has not yet been decided.

– We hope so! If not by us, then maybe by someone else, says Tobias, leaving the door open.

Other exciting projects that competed:

- A physical nightstand drawer that uses four boxes to show whether you should take the train, bus, bike – or whether you can keep sleeping. Based on weather information and public transport departures.
- A simple and clean iOS app that shows the next departure between the places you travel between most often, e.g. work, home and the gym. The app will be released on the AppStore shortly and also works on Apple Watch.
- A VR app for train journeys where you set your arrival and your own location. During the journey, unexpected things pop up…
- An app for seeing where your friends are on a map, which suggests a suitable meeting place based on triangulation from your own position.
- Tryggve – a Facebook chatbot you can chat with when you are on Stockholm's public transport and feel unsafe, scared or tired. Tryggve then tells you what to do. Planned to launch on Facebook shortly.
- An app for connecting with other travellers all over the world – and sharing your travel experiences with each other. With both video and audio.
