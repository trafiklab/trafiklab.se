**This year's four finalists for the Trafiklab Hero 2017 award have been announced. Four individuals behind Res i Stockholm, Trainhack, digital tools for staff in the railway sector and alternative delay forecasts are in the running this year. The winner will be presented at Nordic Open Data Forum on 20 November in Stockholm.**

For the third year, Trafiklab is presenting the Trafiklab Hero of the Year award to recognise the enthusiasts behind digital services that make it easier to travel by public transport.

– The jury has had a tough task selecting the finalists from many high-quality nominations. It is clear that there are many ideas and solutions that, with the help of open data, can significantly develop public transport in Sweden, says Elias Arnestrand, Service Manager Trafiklab.

The four finalists have all contributed to the development of digital services in public transport in Sweden, built on open data or open APIs from Trafiklab. The jury then selects the final winner, who receives the award on 20 November at Nordic Open Data Forum.

**Finalists Trafiklab Hero 2017**

**Ulrika Park**

Through persistent work, Ulrika Park has driven the further development of the Res i Stockholm app, which helps hundreds of thousands of Stockholmers travel by public transport every day. In her work, Ulrika has challenged the industry and contributed constructive development proposals for open APIs made available through Trafiklab.

**The organising team behind Trainhack**

Trainhack is Sweden's first hack on board a train and over two years has developed into an important meeting place for people with a passion for public transport data in Sweden. During the event, ideas and concepts are developed for digital tools that can make travelling by public transport more attractive. With positive energy and a passion for the public transport system, the team organising the event has managed to arrange what many considered impossible, and in 2017 also set a record by organising the world's longest hack on a train.

**Tomas Tydal**

With an incredible passion for the Swedish railway system, Tomas Tydal has contributed greatly to creating digital tools for staff in the railway sector. With creative proposals and ideas, he has challenged the industry's stakeholders and shown in practice how data-driven innovations can make a difference in daily operations on board trains in Sweden.

**Wilhelm Landerholm**

Wilhelm Landerholm is the brain behind the alternative delay forecasts that have been tested for, among others, the commuter train services in Stockholm. Using open data from the railway system as a basis, Willhelm has developed an advanced model that has challenged the industry's traditional way of looking at forecasts and that now forms the basis of the company The Train Brain.
