Last week Commutathon was held, a hackathon with the ambition of making daily commuting easier for Stockholmers, using the mobile phone as a tool. The competition was organised by Sveriges Radio together with Trafiklab, Openlab, Trafik Stockholm, the City of Stockholm and Trafikverket.

During the days, the teams were helped by “Design Thinking” exercises to start from a user perspective and develop a new concept. Through ideation, prototyping and testing, and not least with support from Openlab's coaches, the teams each developed their own proposed solution. They then programmed these concepts as a test version during the rest of the Hackathon. After the Commutathon, one or more concepts will be developed further and one could become something that is tested in Sveriges Radio's digital offering.

The winners of the hack were chosen both by the competition's jury and by Sveriges Radio's listeners. The proposals that won the jury's prize were **Kmutr** and **Trav-Alarm.**

**Kmutr** is an alarm clock that wakes you up when you need to get up in order to cycle to where you're going. If you're too tired you can snooze, and for every snooze your route gets faster, meaning that you for example cycle halfway and take the metro the rest of the way. You don't have to cycle all the way or every day, but you get more points the more you cycle and can compete with your friends.

In **Trav-Alarm** you enter your usual route to work, school, training or wherever you want to go. When something happens that means you can't take your usual route, Trav-Alarm tells you so, and also tells you which alternative route you can take and when you need to leave home to do so. If everything in traffic works as it should - then Trav-Alarm stays silent.

The third team, voted winner on P4 Stockholm's website, is **The Commuters**. The proposal is an app focused on Stockholmers who commute regularly, by car, by bike or by public transport. When the user wants, the app can follow and learn travel habits automatically. By combining static and dynamic open data, as well as information collected jointly by the users, it offers a personal travel assistant and notifications based on the current traffic situation along the route, both as text and speech.
